# Between the Lines: Bhagavad Gita

Welcome to *Between the Lines*, the series where I take you on a journey through the known universe's most important and insightful texts. For the first edition, I am not starting small: introducing the **Bhagavad Gita**.

## Quick Orientation

The Bhagavad Gita (which translates to 'Song of God') is a Hindu scripture that forms a section of the epic poem Mahabharata. It is the most prominent sacred text among the Hindu traditions, and is a central text in the Vedanta tradition - the tradition I will be writing the most about on this blog. In total, the Gita contains 18 chapters with a total of 700 verses. Like most ancient Indian literature, the verses were passed down orally before being commited to writing, but most scholars estimate it was written between 400 and 200 BC by the sage Veda Vyasa.

The story begins with the warrior Arjun, who is on the cusp of victory but has to kill his elders and teachers for triumph. He requests Krishna (basically God) to guide him spiritually through the right path of action - what proceeds is dialog on the nature of the soul, social responsibility, and transcendance from human suffering.

## 3 Major Ideas

> Never was there a time when I did not exist, nor you, nor all these kings; nor in the future shall any of us cease to be.

The Bhagavad Gita is traditionally divided into three sections of six chapters each. To understand these sections, it is important to understand a few simple ideas.
- Yoga is a practical method or spiritual path used to attain union with the divine. It means 'union' or 'to yoke'.
- Suffering is the result of emotional attachment, sorrow, and delusion.
- Self-knowledge is posited as the solution to suffering.

### 1. Karma Yoga

> The enlightened sages call those persons wise, whose every action is free from the desire for material pleasures and who have burnt the reactions of work in the fire of divine knowledge. Such people, having given up attachment to the fruits of their actions, are always satisfied and not dependent on external things. Despite engaging in activities, they do not do anything at all.

The first section is on Karma Yoga, or the Path of Action: performing one's duty seflessly without attachment to the results.

Arjun asks Krisha about which yoga is better, Jnana Yoga (the path of knowledge) or Karma Yoga. If self-knowledge is what liberates us, why do good deeds? Krishna responds that Karma Yoga and Jnana Yoga are complementary to each other and are necessary to complete each other.

A phrase that simplifies the idea behind Karma Yoga: **Proper Action with Proper Attitude**

According to the Bhagavad Gita, how can we have the proper attitude?

1. Look upon your work and actions as worship of God.
2. Take the result of your work as God's gift to you - accepting any result as God's gift.
3. You get to choose between 4 options: you can follow doing work as a commandment from God, you can do work as worship to God, you can use it as a purification ritual, or you can do work because it promotes cosmic order and goodness.

> QUOTE: Work must be done as a yajna to the Supreme Lord; otherwise, work causes bondage in this material world. Therefore, O son of Kunti, for the satisfaction of God, perform your prescribed duties, without being attached to the results.

To put it in simply: do your work as a gift to God, not to chase results, and you are not bound by the results.

### 2. Bhakti Yoga

> QUOTE: Transcendental to this manifest and unmanifest creation, there is yet another unmanifest eternal dimension. That realm does not cease even when all others do. That unmanifest dimension is the supreme goal, and upon reaching it, one never returns to this mortal world. That is My Supreme Abode.

Bhakti Yoga is the spiritual path of love, devotion, and surrender to the divine. You can perform Bhakti in the form of service to the divine, worship or meditation, and Jnana Yoga. **Worship is the goal.**

There are 5 steps to becoming a Bhakta:

1. Develop devotion to God at least for the sake of wordly goals.
2. Make God your ultimate goal.
3. Do not lose sight of God as the goal.
4. Convert every moment of your life into a purifying experience.
5. Surrender to the Lord and do everything with the Lord's blessings.

### Krishna Introduces THIS topic as the ultimate knowledge.

But what is God? Krishna describes God in chapter 9:
> 9.2 "This is the greatest knowledge, the greatest secret, the most sacred, directly attainabloe, unopposed to dharma, very easy to pursue and imperishable" \ 9.4 "All this Universe is pervaded by Me, whose form is unmanifest. All beings are in Me; still, I am not in them."

1. The Real God is all-pervading - any personal god located in a place is an attributed form of God.
2. The Real God is not available for sensory perception. Any divine vision is of attributed form of God alone.
3. The Real God is the support of the Universe.
4. The impurities of the Universe do not affect attributeless God, similar to space not being affected by impurities contained within it.
5. God is the root cause of the origination, existence and dissolution of all creation.
6. The Universe arises in God's presence, he does not do anything, so he is not subject to the karma of the universe. He is therefore a non-doer and non-experiencer.

Arjun asks if it is better to be devoted to a personal, attributed God or the attributeless, infinite supreme God of the Universe. Krishna says it is better to be devoted to a personal God, but both attain the attributeless God.

God has a lower (material) nature and a higher (Consciousness) nature. The Universe is a mixture of God's higher and lower nature. Krishna wants us to change our attitude, to see divinity everywhere and develop reverence for everything - seeing the Universe as "The Lord's Cosmic Form". Here are benefits to viewing Creation in this manner:

- I will stop naturally hating anyone or anything as they are all God's forms.
- I become hunble because I see that any glory of mine is actually the Lord's.
- Everything is sacred to the enlightened one.
- My thinking expands; I become open-minded by seeing the vastness of Creation.

### 3. Jnana Yoga

> "Just as one sun illumines the entire solar system, so does the individual soul illumine the entire body (with consciousness)."

Jnana Yoga is the path of knowledge. First one studies, then one is convinced of the information, then one assimilates it to memory and practice. This refers specifically to scripture but can be anything you want.

**Benefits of Jnana Yoga**
- Ability to see everything as the same, like seeing bot hthe wave and ocean as water.
- Allows me to do my duty to the best of my ability without regard for the results.
- The essential nature of Self and God are the same, this knowledge gives me liberation while I am still alive.

14.24 "The wise man, abiding in self, is the same in pleasure and pain. He is the same towards a clod of earth, a stone or gold. He is the same in desirable and undesirable situations. He is the same in censure and praise of himself (by others)."

**Preparing for Self Knowledge**
1. Refining myself as a human being.
    - I should take care of my physical, emotional, and intellectual health.
    - Proper diet + exercise.
    - Intellectual health = intellect capable of learning, clear thought and communication.
2. Refining myself as a doer of my free will.
    - As a doer, all my actions should be deliberate, thoughtful and well considered. I can refine myself further by choosing noble or sattvic actions.
3. Refining myself as an experiencer and not being attached to outcomes.
    - I can improve as an experiencer by having the proper reaction to my experiences, accepting choiceless situations calmly and strengthening my mind to endure without any damage.
4. Refining myself as a seeker.
    - As a seeker, cultivate faith in God, teacher and scripture.

As a summary, Jnana Yoga consists of study, conviction, and assimilation of the knowledge.
